IIPP internship in Taiwan: finding and emailing a professor

The International Internship Pilot Program (IIPP) is run by Taiwan's National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) and brings international students to Taiwanese research labs. Finding a host professor is a key step.

What IIPP is

IIPP is a research internship program in Taiwan supported by the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC). Universities and partner institutions publish IIPP calls each year with their own deadlines and conditions; the official site is iipp.tw. Always read the current call before applying.

How to find a host professor

  • Look at research groups at participating Taiwanese universities in your field.
  • Choose professors whose recent papers or projects match your experience. Fit matters more than university ranking.
  • Collect name, email, research area and university in a spreadsheet.

Sample email for IIPP

Subject: IIPP internship inquiry, [Your name]

Dear Professor [Surname],

My name is [Your name], a [degree, year] student at [University]. I would like to ask whether you might consider hosting me through the International Internship Pilot Program (IIPP) from [month] to [month] [year].

Your work on [research area] is close to my project on [one concrete thing you did].

I have attached my CV and a short research proposal. I am happy to send further documents if useful.

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
[Your name]

After sending

Allow about 5 days, then follow up once in the same thread. If a professor declines or points you to a colleague, thank them; a polite reply keeps the door open.

Questions

Who runs IIPP?

The International Internship Pilot Program is supported by Taiwan's National Science and Technology Council (NSTC). Participating universities publish their own calls and deadlines.

Do I need a host professor for IIPP?

Students generally need a professor in Taiwan willing to host them, so contacting suitable professors early is important. Check the current call for exact requirements.

Can I apply to TEEP and IIPP at the same time?

They are separate programs with separate calls. Read each call's rules; you can contact professors about either, but say clearly which program you are applying through.
