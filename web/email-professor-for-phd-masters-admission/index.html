Emailing a professor for PhD or master's admission

For many research degrees, contacting a potential supervisor before you apply is expected, and sometimes required. A focused email shows you have read their work and know what you want to study.

What to include

  • The degree and intake you are applying for (for example, PhD, Fall 2027).
  • Your background in one or two lines: degree, main project, any publication.
  • How your interests fit their research: name a specific topic or paper of theirs.
  • Funding: mention a scholarship you are applying for, or ask whether funded positions exist.
  • Attachments: your CV and a short research proposal.

Sample email

Subject: Prospective PhD student, [topic], [Your name]

Dear Professor [Surname],

My name is [Your name]. I recently completed my [degree] at [University], and I am applying for the PhD program in [department] for the [intake] intake.

Your work on [specific topic] relates closely to my project on [what you did], and I would like to continue in this direction under your supervision. I am also applying for the [scholarship] scholarship.

I have attached my CV and a short research proposal. Could you let me know whether you are taking new students for this intake?

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
[Your name]

Tips

  • Write to professors whose recent work you can describe in a sentence. Generic praise is easy to spot.
  • One email per professor in the same department is enough. Don't copy several professors on one email.
  • Follow up once after about 5 days if there is no reply.

Questions

Should I email a professor before applying for a PhD?

In many countries and departments, yes: contacting a potential supervisor first is expected and can be required. Check the department's admission page.

What should I attach when emailing a potential supervisor?

Your CV and a short research proposal. Offer transcripts and recommendation letters on request.

Should I mention scholarships in the email?

Yes, briefly: name the scholarship you are applying for, or ask whether funded positions are available.
